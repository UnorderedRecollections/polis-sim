# Constitutional Approval Act

## Preamble

Whereas the constitution is enforced by people, and no text may enter the
federal archive except through the common procedure; and whereas required
approval has until now rested in custom alone: be it enacted that the
rules of required approval are codified, so that the archive itself
refuses what the constitution forbids.

## Article 1 — Purpose

This Act entrenches required approval: no change may enter the federal
archive except by the procedure herein named, and the platform shall
refuse every change the procedure does not admit.

## Article 2 — Provisions

1. The mainline of the federal archive may be advanced only by the Keeper
   of the Federal Rolls (office: `federal-archivist`).
2. The mainline of a city's archive (`municipal/<city>/**`) may be
   advanced only by that city's archivist (office:
   `local-archivist-<city>`).
3. No proposal may be ratified without constitutional review by the
   Constitutional Council; a proposal the Council finds incompatible with
   the constitution shall not be ratified.
4. The Rules of Required Approval are annexed to this Act as Schedule 1
   (the branch-protection instrument).

## Article 3 — Enforcement

The platform realizes these rules mechanically by protecting the branches
herein named. The platform's protection records the law; it does not make
it — the legal effect rests with the offices, and a breach remains a
breach whether or not the platform detected it.

## Article 4 — Relation to Existing Law

This Act gives effect to Article 2 of the founding constitution (custom
until altered by the common procedure) and to the schedule of authority
established by the Assigned Legal Authority Act.

## Article 5 — Entry into Force

This Act enters into force upon its ratification by the Keeper of the
Federal Rolls.

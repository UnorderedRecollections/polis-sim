# Political Economy — three layers (§17–§20)

The story distinguishes three things that are easy to conflate:

## 1. Material substrate

Machines, networks, energy, hardware. Scarce, capital-intensive, materially
necessary — and **not part of the software commons**. (In the simulation:
the podman host, the containers, postgres.)

## 2. Knowledge/archival commons

Git, open-source tools, protocols, shared legal knowledge. Reproducible and
largely non-rivalrous; producible through cooperative activity outside
conventional market exchange.

## 3. Institutional/platform layer

The GitHub-like Federal Hall. It can be privately owned and governed even
though built around an open commons:

> **A commons can remain technically open while the dominant institution for
> coordinating around it is privately controlled.**

## The deeper relationship (§20)

Not simply "capital captures the commons":

```
MATERIAL SUBSTRATE → COMMONS → INSTITUTIONAL LAYER → economic production
```

The commons can be **productive without being privately owned**,
**appropriated without being enclosed**, and **used by private organizations
without becoming their property**. Because software is non-rivalrous, the
same common artifact can remain freely available to everyone while being
incorporated into privately organized production.

## Licenses — conditions of legal inheritance (§17)

A software license is a condition attached to a body of law concerning how it
may be inherited, modified and redistributed. Copyleft in particular: a legal
inheritance condition requiring downstream legal orders to preserve specified
freedoms. The conditions **travel with the inherited material**.
